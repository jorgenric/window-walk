# 0001: Eligibility covers any independent shop selling goods available from online mass retailers

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Decided by: Eric
- Settles: part of C-02 (what counts as an eligible goods seller; verification is still open)

## Context

The concept's eligibility rule, "local businesses selling goods that compete with remote sellers," left gray areas. Sorting real Parkrose-area shops turned up independent grocers, auto-parts sellers, appliance and flooring stores, and variety stores that didn't clearly fit the "independent retail" examples.

## Decision

Any independent shop that sells home goods a household could otherwise buy from Amazon or another online mass retailer fits the purpose of the service.

In practice:

- **In:** independent hardware, home, kitchen, furniture, flooring and appliance stores; independent grocers and ethnic markets (packaged goods); variety and general stores; auto and bike parts; electronics, phones and computers; clothing, gifts, books, toys, pet supplies; used and vintage goods.
- **Needs a closer look:** shops selling mainly fresh food (bakeries, butchers, delis), restricted goods (tobacco, vape, alcohol), and service-led shops (opticians, tire shops, florists). These qualify when a meaningful share of what they sell is online-substitutable.
- **Out:** chains and franchises, services, and goods that can't be bought online (cannabis, prepared meals).

## Consequences

- Many more shops qualify, especially in outer neighborhoods where specialty retail is sparse and independent grocers and suppliers are the local alternative to online shopping.
- The "character over catalog" principle carries more weight: a grocer or parts store needs a good story and photos to make browsing feel like window shopping.
- "Independent" still has to be verified (C-02), since some chains aren't obvious from their names.
